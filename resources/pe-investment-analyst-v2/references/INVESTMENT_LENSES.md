# Focused investment lenses — read only when applicable

This is a research aid, not a required checklist. Follow the actual business's economics. These methods are adapted to post-investment monitoring from public PE skill repositories; see the package-level `SOURCES_AND_CHANGES.md` for specific attributions.

## Evidence ladder

Give greater weight to original executed agreements, bank/ledger records and third-party confirmations than to management commentary. Within a single workbook, system-originated detail and reconcilable account schedules provide stronger numerical support than unaudited prose claims. Record source conflicts instead of silently choosing the more convenient figure. A referenced contract in management commentary isn't the actual contract.

## Verification chains (stop wherever evidence stops)

- **Revenue quality:** contract/pricing schedule → service or usage → invoice → revenue recognition → AR/WIP → collection. If only management accounts exist, state that invoices and receipts weren't reviewed. Explain gross customer attrition independently of upsell-driven net retention. For acquisitions, use genuinely comparable legacy bases and acquisition dates; an all-company prior-year comparison is not organic growth.
- **Margins:** reported revenue → price × volume × mix where usable → labor/material/supplier/service cost → segment contribution → corporate overhead → EBITDA. Do not claim a particular driver explains all of a margin miss unless a full variance bridge supports it.
- **Earnings quality:** locate each add-back's amount, date, cash/noncash nature, evidence, recurrence and completion status. One-time ≠ recurring monthly. A prospective salary reset or synergy does not equal realized savings. Excluding questioned adjustments yields a *sensitivity*, not an auditor-approved QoE number.
- **Cash and capex:** EBITDA → operating cash flow → working capital → cash capex → noncash financing of asset purchases/finance leases → debt/lease principal and net debt. An economically adjusted reinvestment calculation may be different from the financial-statement FCF line. Do not double count lease capex or principal; describe the convention being compared.
- **Capacity and contractual obligations:** reported output/revenue → labor/equipment/assets needed → utilization/downtime/maintenance → customer pricing and escalator limitations → funded reinvestment plan. An unfunded operational recovery should not be assumed to occur just because management predicts it.
- **Forecast:** historical trend → conversion/pipeline/bookings → capacity and price realization → expected cash funding. Challenge rosy statements, but do not pronounce a forecast impossible without evidence.

## What a GP actually needs from a finding

1. The **size** and definition of the exposure (EBITDA, revenue at renewal, capital requirement, liquidity headroom, or not quantifiable).
2. The **source quality** and disputed assumption.
3. The **concentration/correlation**: several bad metrics may come from the same customer, segment, acquisition or asset fleet. Avoid double counting.
4. The **decision path**: Act now (time sensitive), Investigate (missing critical proof), Monitor (tracked leading indicator), Opportunity (identified upside).
5. A **specific next step**: requested schedule, contractual evidence, management action, downside sensitivity or credible mitigation; propose a responsible *role* if appropriate. Don't invent a named owner.

When proposing remediation, distinguish one-off cash investment from recurring annual EBITDA impact, and identify timing and confidence where support exists. If the company has an existing value-creation plan, compare actual progress to its claimed benefits, milestones and leading KPIs. Never manufacture a plan that wasn't supplied.

## KPIs without dashboard bloat

For company monitoring, select **8–12 meaningful indicators at most** only if designing an ongoing scorecard; a one-time analysis need not invent a full scorecard. Each KPI should have: definition (numerator/denominator), source, reporting cut-off, comparable reference (budget / prior year / trend), and proposed escalation criterion. Thresholds should be sponsor-approved and tailored to the business, not arbitrary universal 5%/15% color rules. A good indicator warns early enough to change an action; if a metric cannot inform a decision or test a value-creation lever, demote it.

Monthly meeting outputs should be decisions and unresolved escalations, not another slideshow reading every KPI. Board-level strategy and capital decisions belong in a separate quarterly narrative where appropriate.

## Blind-test precautions

Don't rely on company names, specific fabricated benchmarks, answer keys or earlier experiment outputs. Inspect novel source material on its own terms. Preserve failed/unverified candidate claims separately from accepted conclusions. Do not count plausible unsupported observations as successful findings.
